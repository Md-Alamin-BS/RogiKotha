The chosen direction, and the staff console beside it.

Two artefacts, one design system, the same fictional records and the same product rules. 04b Quiet Clinic (minimal) is the chosen patient direction (FD-027, 2026-09-06): paper base, teal-gray accent, hairline rules, small radii, no serif/sans contrast, CSS-only motion. The staff console is a separate portal on its own surface system (Chart Tray): four planes where elevation encodes agency, so a moderator can see what is workable and what is read-only before reading a word. Six earlier patient directions (01, 02, 03, 04, 04c, 04d) and the three admin shell studies that produced Chart Tray are kept in .archive/ (on branch archive/full-project-2026-11-09) — the record of how these two were arrived at, not live work.

What the product actually is

RogiKotha is a Bangladesh-first, bilingual platform where patients, guardians, caregivers and treatment witnesses publish first-hand accounts of a single treatment episode — the symptoms, the diagnosis, the medicines, what happened afterwards.

It is deliberately narrative-first. There are no star ratings, no calculated quality score and no best-to-worst leaderboard, because the platform refuses to reduce a person's treatment to a number. That single constraint is what makes this an interesting design problem: how do you make a directory feel decisive without a 4.8 ★?

The answer this product settled on is named on the card below.

Rules every direction obeys

  • No number ever stands for quality — only Positive / Mixed / Negative distributions with exact counts and denominators.
  • Limited data whenever the sample is sparse.
  • Missing values read Not provided or No documents, never 0.
  • Ordering is neutral: relevance, volume or recency. There is deliberately no "highest rated" sort.
  • Registration not verified on every doctor — Phase 1 has no BMDC verification.
  • Evidence reads Documents attached — not medically validated. Never "verified".
  • User claims render as Reviewer's label, never as a platform finding.
  • Facility feedback never touches a doctor's summaries.
  • Bangla and English are peers, with a live toggle. Light and dark in both.
  • Motion can be switched off without losing a single fact.

How to read it

  1. Walk the screens in order. The one that matters most is Account — it is where the product actually lives.
  2. Hit the অ button. Bengali has no capitals and needs far more leading, so the type scale is rebuilt for the script rather than swapping strings into a Latin one. Numerals switch too. This is where most "beautiful" designs quietly break.
  3. Hit ◐ for the other colour mode, then ◌ to kill motion. Nothing should become unreadable or disappear. In the console, watch the surface steps: dark uses roughly 1.7× the light step, because a tint that works on paper all but vanishes on near-black.
  4. Narrow the window to phone width. Margins should fold under the text, not vanish.
  5. Then ask the real question: on the doctor page, do you feel able to decide anything? That is the whole bet of a product with no star ratings.
