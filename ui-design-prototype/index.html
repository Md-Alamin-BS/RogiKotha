RogiKotha

Three directions on the same product.

Each renders the same fictional records and obeys the same product rules, so what differs between them is only the design. 03 is the warm editorial direction and the only one carrying the moderation console. 04 is the calm clinic — oat and muted teal, serif headings, generous air — built from researched healthcare-identity conventions (FD-026). 04b is the chosen implementation direction (FD-027, 2026-09-06): the same clinic posture, expressed more quietly — paper instead of oat, teal-gray instead of teal, hairline rules, smaller radii, no serif/sans contrast, CSS-only motion. Two sibling variants (04c Editorial, 04d Modern SaaS) were explored during the side-by-side and archived.

What the product actually is

RogiKotha is a Bangladesh-first, bilingual platform where patients, guardians, caregivers and treatment witnesses publish first-hand accounts of a single treatment episode — the symptoms, the diagnosis, the medicines, what happened afterwards.

It is deliberately narrative-first. There are no star ratings, no calculated quality score and no best-to-worst leaderboard, because the platform refuses to reduce a person's treatment to a number. That single constraint is what makes this an interesting design problem: how do you make a directory feel decisive without a 4.8 ★?

Each direction answers that question differently. The answer is named on every card below.

Rules every direction obeys

  • No number ever stands for quality — only Positive / Mixed / Negative distributions with exact counts and denominators.
  • Limited data whenever the sample is sparse.
  • Missing values read Not provided or No documents, never 0.
  • Ordering is neutral: relevance, volume or recency. There is deliberately no "highest rated" sort.
  • Registration not verified on every doctor — Phase 1 has no BMDC verification.
  • Evidence reads Documents attached — not medically validated. Never "verified".
  • User claims render as Reviewer's label, never as a platform finding.
  • Facility feedback never touches a doctor's summaries.
  • Bangla and English are peers, with a live toggle. Light and dark in both.
  • Motion can be switched off without losing a single fact.

DiscoverSearch, browse by condition with counts, emergency guidance, a way into facilities.
ResultsDoctors / Facilities tabs, filters, neutral sort, and a compare tray — pin up to three.

How to compare them

  1. Walk the screens in order. The one that matters most is Account — it is where the product actually lives.
  2. Hit the অ button. Bengali has no capitals and needs far more leading, so each direction rebuilds its type scale for the script rather than swapping strings into a Latin one. Numerals switch too. This is where most "beautiful" designs quietly break.
  3. Hit ◐ for the other colour mode, then ◌ to kill motion. Nothing should become unreadable or disappear.
  4. Narrow the window to phone width. Margins should fold under the text, not vanish.
  5. Then ask the real question: on the doctor page, do you feel able to decide anything? That is the whole bet of a product with no star ratings.

No build step and no dependencies. Open this file directly, or serve the folder with python -m http.server 4173. Nothing loads from the network.

Everything shown is fictional and synthetic. No real doctor, facility, patient, BMDC number or document is represented. Provider marks are deterministic generated geometry derived from a name hash — never a photograph or likeness of a person.
